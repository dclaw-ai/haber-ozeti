Top 5 of the week

Iran war: Hormuz attacks, mediators and a reopening plan

Tankers were reportedly hit in Hormuz all week while Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish officials went to Tehran and Iran put forward a seven-day reopening plan. By Sunday it was a two-day plan with Qatar, and Tehran said the tone of a US letter had changed.

Israel's election ends in deadlock and Likud claims it was stolen

Exit polls showed neither bloc reaching 61 seats after record turnout, and the final count left Netanyahu's bloc at 53 of 120. His party then said the election was stolen, which election officials see no basis for.

Binance suffers a record $6.3 billion hack blamed on North Korea

Binance paused withdrawals on suspected multibillion-dollar losses. Analysts later blamed North Korean hackers and said about $3 billion had been frozen.

Ukraine: Oleshky ceasefire evacuations, a deadly Dnipro strike and Patriot missiles

A Russian drone strike killed at least 14 at Dnipro station while a short local ceasefire let civilians leave Oleshky. The US then approved a $2.1 billion sale of 500 Patriot missiles to Ukraine.

Best AI project of the week

Multi-agent systems

Spawn: a multiplayer game run by agents that ship to production hourly

The founder describes a game where players prompt an in-game AI, Savi, to reshape the world, while a swarm of agents edits live code and ships to production hourly. Savi files bugs herself and an agent triages each one, with monitors waking an agent that owns the incident and rollbacks taking 30 seconds. He says it costs over $3 million in tokens a month; the blogger who covered it doubts the approach would suit higher-stakes software.
